Polynomial Boundary Coordinates for Collatz Cycles

Aaron Kyle Solomon

Restrictions on candidate parity patterns for positive integer cycles of the shortcut Collatz map: when the pattern length and number of odd symbols are coprime, polynomial boundary coordinates express integral realizability as a divisibility condition and give a square-root-scale lower bound on boundary support. The analysis recovers Knight’s high-cycle exclusion and rules out every word obtained from the mechanical word by a single adjacent 01→10 swap.

Abstract

This paper studies arithmetic obstructions to realizing prescribed parity patterns as positive integer cycles of the shortcut Collatz map. A binary parity word of length n with u odd symbols determines a rational periodic orbit, but the standard numerator formula does not reveal how the arrangement of those symbols affects integrality. Toward characterizing this dependence, we develop coordinates that measure a prescribed parity pattern relative to the corresponding upper-Christoffel mechanical word. The construction is developed in the positive cycle-relevant range u<n<2u and 2n>3u. Under the additional condition gcd(n,u)=1, the map t↦(n−u)t mod u permutes the u odd phases and places them in a lossless residue order. Each cyclic parity class then has a unique rotation whose cumulative zero path lies below the balanced mechanical path of the corresponding upper-Christoffel representative. The nonnegative vertical displacement between these paths is called the bridge L. Writing its heights in residue order and taking a discrete boundary produces a sparse polynomial QL whose occupied coefficients are exactly the K(L) plateaux of the reordered bridge.

The rational orbit is integral exactly when QL is divisible by 2Xn−u−3 in ℤ[X]/(Xu−2). When this holds, the profile quotient recovers the odd orbit and the boundary quotient has full support. Integral realizability therefore turns a sparse geometric boundary into a dense arithmetic response. Only the K(L) occupied coefficients of QL can interrupt the induced quotient recurrence: a long intervening gap forces exponential 3-adic growth, while bridge geometry bounds the sum of the absolute boundary coefficients in terms of K(L) and Laurent’s estimate controls the denominator loss. Balancing these constraints yields the uniform bound K(L)≥√log23 √u−O((log u)2) for hypothetical coprime positive cycles as u→∞. Thus such a cycle cannot lie in the low-boundary-complexity regime below the square-root scale.

Finally, the analysis recovers Knight’s high-cycle exclusion and excludes every one-swap mechanical word from encoding a nontrivial positive integer Collatz cycle.

From a parity word to its boundary polynomial

In the coprime setting, the construction passes from a cyclic parity word to a sparse polynomial in four steps.

  1. 01

    Canonical root and bridge

    Select the unique rotation lying below the balanced mechanical reference. Their vertical separation records the bridge.

  2. 02

    Residue order

    Place the powers of two and three in the cycle numerator on a common exponent scale, then order the bridge heights by the resulting residues. Coprimality ensures that every odd phase occurs exactly once.

  3. 03

    Dyadic profile

    Translate the reordered bridge heights into powers of two. Consecutive equal values form constant intervals, or plateaux.

  4. 04

    Boundary polynomial

    Take the reduced discrete boundary of the profile. The resulting polynomial has one nonzero coefficient for each plateau and retains the profile information.

Example (8, 5, 3)

Here (n,u,z)=(8,5,3), with z=n−u. For this small nonintegral illustration of the coordinate construction, we take the arrangement 11101100 of five ones and three zeros. Its cyclic class includes other rotations, such as 11001110. The rotation 11101100 is canonical because, before each successive 1, it has encountered no more zeros than the mechanical reference word 11011010. By contrast, 11001110 fails before its third 1: it has already encountered two zeros, while the mechanical reference has encountered one. At each odd phase, the difference between these cumulative zero counts is the bridge height; for the canonical rotation, L=(0,0,1,0,1,0).

Since z=3, the residue labels in odd-phase order are 3t mod 5=(0,3,1,4,2). Increasing residue order therefore reads the phases as t=(0,2,4,1,3), so the corresponding bridge heights are H=(0,1,1,0,0). Their maximum is M=1, and the dyadic profile values are therefore

dr=21−Hr,  d=(2,1,1,2,2).

Writing these coefficients in reverse residue powers gives the profile polynomial

VL(X) =2X4+X3+X2+2X+2.

Its reduced discrete boundary is obtained by multiplying by X−1 and then using X5=2:

QL(X) =[(X−1)VL(X)]X5=2
=[2X5−X4+X2−2]X5=2
=2+X2−X4.
