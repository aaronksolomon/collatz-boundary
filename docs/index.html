Polynomial Boundary Coordinates for Collatz Cycles

This manuscript develops polynomial boundary coordinates for prescribed parity patterns of the shortcut Collatz map. Under the standing coprime positive-cycle hypotheses, these coordinates give an exact criterion for integral realizability and an asymptotic lower bound on residue-order plateau complexity.

Necessary asymptotic condition · Theorem 1.2

lim infj→∞ K(Lj)/√uj ≥ √log23

For every sequence of nontrivial positive Collatz cycles with least periods nj, odd-member counts uj, gcd(nj, uj)=1, and uj→∞, the canonical bridges Lj satisfy this bound.

Thus any sufficiently large cycle in the coprime regime would require on the order of √u residue-order plateaux.

Scope

Under u<n<2u, 2n>3u, and gcd(n,u)=1, integral realizability is reduced to divisibility of the sparse boundary QL by 2Xn−u−3 in ℤ[X]/(Xu−2). Along any sequence of hypothetical nontrivial positive cycles in this coprime regime with u→∞, Theorem 1.2 gives the stated asymptotic lower bound on K(L)/√u.

Manuscript

Abstract

This paper studies how integrality constrains the shape of parity patterns for hypothetical cycles of the shortcut Collatz map. A binary parity word of length n with u odd symbols determines a rational periodic orbit, but the standard numerator formula does not reveal how the arrangement of those symbols affects integrality. The construction is developed in the positive cycle-relevant range u<n<2u and 2n>3u. Under the additional condition gcd(n,u)=1, the map t↦(n−u)t mod u permutes the u odd phases and places them in a lossless residue order. Each cyclic parity class then has a unique rotation whose cumulative zero path lies below the balanced mechanical path of the corresponding upper-Christoffel representative. The nonnegative vertical displacement between these paths is called the bridge L. Writing its heights in residue order and taking a discrete boundary produces a sparse polynomial QL whose occupied coefficients are exactly the K(L) plateaux of the reordered bridge.

The rational orbit is integral exactly when QL is divisible by 2Xn−u−3 in ℤ[X]/(Xu−2). When this holds, the profile quotient recovers the odd orbit, while the boundary quotient occupies every residue position. Integrality therefore becomes a tension between a sparse geometric boundary and a dense arithmetic quotient: long boundary gaps force 3-adic growth, while the bridge geometry gives a competing dyadic upper bound. Combining these estimates yields K(L)≥(√log23−o(1))√u along any sequence of hypothetical coprime positive cycles with u→∞. Thus a large cycle cannot have o(√u) residue-order plateaux. The same coordinates recover Knight’s mechanical-word exclusion, and a separate argument excludes every singleton bridge candidate whenever 1≤u<n and 2n>3u.

Construction

From parity word to sparse boundary

Exact transformations separate cyclic ambiguity, geometric displacement, arithmetic position, and boundary complexity.

  1. 01

    Canonical bridge

    Root the cyclic word uniquely below its balanced mechanical reference and record the vertical displacement L.

  2. 02

    Residue order

    Use rt=(n−u)t mod u to place every odd phase on the arithmetic exponent scale.

  3. 03

    Dyadic profile

    Convert reordered bridge heights into positive powers of two; constant height intervals become literal plateaux.

  4. 04

    Sparse boundary

    Take the reduced discrete boundary in ℤ[X]/(Xu−2). Its support size is exactly the plateau count.

Example

The example (8, 5, 3)

Here (n,u,z)=(8,5,3), with z=n−u. This small nonintegral example is used only to illustrate the coordinate construction; it is not a Collatz cycle. The mechanical reference word is 11011010. The canonically rooted comparison word 11101100 has bridge L=(0,0,1,0,1,0).

Code companion

Coordinate reproduction

A small standard-library Python package validates an already rooted legal bridge and computes every displayed coordinate in the (8,5,3) example. It does not search for cycles or silently canonicalize an arbitrary word.
